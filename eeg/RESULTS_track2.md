# THOR EEG-MI — NeuroBench Track-2 accounting (SynOps + footprint)

Leaderboard-style comparison of our shallow spike-rate net against the reproduced official deep
baseline, scored under the NeuroBench Track-2 convention. Generated by
[`probe_synops.py`](probe_synops.py) (op counter) on top of the spike-native covariance net in
[`probe_spike_cov.py`](probe_spike_cov.py). Accuracies are the val numbers reported in
[`SIGNAL_LEAK_CLOSEOUT.md`](SIGNAL_LEAK_CLOSEOUT.md).

> **Scope note.** This Track-2 win is a property of *this task's* shallow, second-order (band-power)
> structure — not a general SNN advance, and deliberately **not** part of the Paper 3 headline (which
> leads on the forwards-only jug's intrinsic ~0.04 gap to Adam). It is included here for completeness
> so the leaderboard comparison is reproducible from the repository.

## Track-2 convention

A connection driven by a **continuous** input costs a dense MAC per application; a connection driven
by **spikes** costs one AC per actual presynaptic spike (measured at the trained firing rate).
Footprint = weight count. All counts are **per sample** at `T = 250` timesteps.

- **deep** (official `62→256→128→2`, all LIF): `fc1` continuous EEG in → MACs `62·256·T`; `fc2`
  spikes in → ACs `(L1 spikes/sample)·128`; `fc3` spikes in → ACs `(L2 spikes/sample)·2`.
- **shallow** (`62→16` LIF → rate → head `16→2`): `W0` continuous EEG in → MACs `62·16·T`; head
  pooled rates in → MACs `16·2` (once per sample).

## Result (per sample, T = 250)

| model | acc | dense MACs | spike ACs | total ops | footprint (weights) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| deep baseline (62→256→128→2) | 0.653 | 3,968,000 | 384,153 | **4,352,153** | **48,896** |
| shallow spike-rates (62→16→2) | 0.682 | 248,032 | 0 | **248,032** | **1,024** |
| **ratio (deep / shallow)** | **+0.029** | | | **17.5×** | **47.8×** |

Firing rates: deep L1 4.7% / L2 1.2%; shallow 9.3%. The deep cost is dominated by `fc1`'s **dense
MACs** (continuous EEG into 256 neurons); the shallow net is 16× narrower there and has no deep
layers ⇒ **17.5× fewer ops, 47.8× smaller — and +0.029 more accurate.** The operative feature is the
per-filter spike-rate power (the "square"); depth and cross-terms are not needed.

## Why the diagonal is the whole signal

Building the covariance *from spikes* and ablating cross-terms shows the off-diagonal coincidences add
noise, not information (which is *why* CSP is a diagonal of filtered variance):

| ablation (`probe_spike_cov.py`, K=16, 60 ep) | best val |
|---|---|
| **`rates`** (spike rates, diagonal) | **0.682** |
| `coinc` (coincidences only) | 0.659 |
| `full` (rates ⊕ coincidences) | 0.663 |
| `cont_cov` (continuous full covariance) | 0.698 |
| *(ref: continuous diagonal square/energy)* | *0.710* |

⇒ no K² coincidence machinery is needed; the shallow diagonal net is both cheaper and better.
